Settle three things before the first pipe goes in

Every install starts with where the water comes from, where it drains and whether the city needs to know. A new fixture beside an existing one is simple. A new fixture across the house means running line, and that changes the time, the permit and the price.

We walk the space with you and look at the basement ceiling, the panel of shutoffs and the nearest drain. Then we tell you what the job involves in plain terms. If something should be fixed first, such as a tired supply line, we say so before you spend money on top of it.

Local note

Basement bathrooms and Solon's sewer rules

Solon's code, Chapter 1042, says foundation drains may not be tied into the sanitary sewer, and sanitary sewers are not meant to carry ground or storm water. If you finish a basement, your new bathroom drain and your foundation drain are separate systems. Mixing them up can mean a failed inspection and a costly redo.

The same chapter puts the cost of building sewer work on the owner, and it limits permits for opening or altering sewers to registered sewer installers. Many homes sit lower than the street sewer, so a basement bath may need an ejector pump. We tell you which applies to your house before you buy fixtures. If the real trouble is the lateral outside, that is a sewer line repair conversation.

Check who is working in your house

Solon requires contractors to register with the city each year and, for plumbers, to show a current Ohio license. The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board runs a free online look-up, so you can confirm any plumber in a few minutes. We encourage you to look us up.

The 2024 Ohio Plumbing Code took effect on March 1, 2024, and Solon has adopted the Ohio Plumbing Code in its city code. If you plan to dig outside for a new line, call 811 at least two working days before. Ohio811 is free and applies even to hand digging.
