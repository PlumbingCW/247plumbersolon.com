Water around the tank: what to do before we get there

These steps take a couple of minutes and keep a small leak from becoming a flooded utility room.

  • ✓Close the cold supply.The shutoff valve sits on the pipe feeding the top of the tank. Turn the handle until it stops. That ends new water reaching a leaking shell.
  • ✓Cut the power.On an electric unit, switch off its breaker. On a gas unit, turn the control to off or pilot. An electric element left on in an emptied tank burns out quickly.
  • ✓Smell gas? Leave.Do not touch switches or use a phone inside. Get everyone outdoors and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Call us once the building is safe.
  • ✓Protect the floor.Move boxes, stored clothes, and cardboard away from the water. Towels or a shop vacuum at the edge keep it out of finished rooms.

If water keeps appearing while the heater stays dry, the source may be a supply line, which is a job for leak detection. Tell us whether the water comes from the seam, the top pipes, or the relief valve drain.

Hot water trouble and what it often points to

No hot water at all

On electric units a tripped breaker or a dead element is common. On gas units the pilot or thermocouple is a frequent suspect. Many of these are quick repairs.

Warm, but never hot

One failed element or a burner buried in sediment can leave the tank half heated. Showers start fine and fade to lukewarm.

Rumbling or popping

Sediment at the bottom traps steam bubbles as the water heats. An early flush helps. A thick crust may mean the tank is past it.

Rusty hot water only

If the cold side runs clear, the tank lining or anode rod is likely corroding. Treat it as a warning about the tank's remaining life.

A puddle underneath

A loose fitting, a weeping relief valve, and a rusted shell all leave water on the floor. Each one calls for a different fix.

Hot water that runs out fast

A broken dip tube lets cold water mix in near the top of the tank. It is a modest repair on a tank that is otherwise sound.

Local note

What Solon's water does to a tank

Cleveland Water supplies Solon from Lake Erie and describes its water as about 7 grains per gallon, which it calls moderately hard. Hardness minerals settle as scale on heating elements and in the bottom of a tank. So when a Solon heater runs cold or rumbles, scale is a fair first suspect.

If your home has a water softener, tell us on the call. Softening changes how the anode rod wears, so we inspect it with that in mind. A tap test with a hardness strip tells you where your own house stands.
