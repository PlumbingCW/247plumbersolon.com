Sewer Camera Inspection in Solon, OH

A sewer camera inspection in Solon, OH shows the inside of your drain line on video, so you can see roots, cracks, sags or buildup instead of guessing. Solon 24/7 Plumbing runs the camera, explains each finding in plain words, and gives you a clear price before any repair is discussed.

The idea

A look inside the pipe beats a guess

A drain cable tells you something is in the way. It cannot tell you whether that something is a wad of roots, a cracked joint or a pipe that has sagged. A small waterproof camera on a flexible rod can. If the pipe only needs clearing, our drain cleaning crews handle that too.

We feed it in through a cleanout, a pulled toilet or another access point and watch the picture live. You can watch with us. The goal is a plain answer to one question: is this pipe clean and sound, or does it need work?

Good reasons to put a camera in the line

Repeat clogs

When a line keeps backing up after cleaning, the cause is usually a defect the cable never showed.

A cable that stalled

If an earlier plumber hit something at a certain distance, the camera shows what it was.

Buying or selling a home

A video of the lateral shows condition before you commit, and it gives sellers something to work from.

Wet spots or sunken ground in the yard

A break can leak sewage into the soil. The camera helps tell a pipe problem from a drainage problem.

Before a big plumbing project

Checking the line first keeps surprises out of a remodel or a repair estimate.

How the inspection runs

  1. 1

    Pick the access point

    A cleanout is best. If there is none, we may work through a toilet, which takes more time.

  2. 2

    Clear loose debris if needed

    A camera cannot see through grease or standing waste, so we sometimes run a quick cleaning pass first.

  3. 3

    Record the run

    We guide the camera along the pipe, pausing at joints, bends and anything unusual, and note about how far in each finding sits.

  4. 4

    Review it with you

    We show you the footage, explain what each image means and what options exist, from doing nothing to a repair.

A camera shows what is inside the pipe it can reach. It does not predict future failures, and tight bends or a collapsed section can stop it from going farther.

Local note

Why age matters for Solon laterals

Census survey data from 2016 put the median home in Solon at 1981 vintage, and nearly half of the homes were built before 1980. A lateral that is decades old has had decades to collect roots, shift or crack.

That is not a measure of any one house, and your own line may be fine. The city says the property owner is responsible for the lateral from the house to the cleanout, so a video of that stretch is worth having before you decide on work.

What happens after the video

Not every finding calls for a repair. A line with light root fibers might only need cleaning every so often, while a broken section needs a real fix. We give you both options and what each means for your wallet and your house.

If a dig is needed, Ohio811 says anyone digging must notify it at least two working days before digging, and a contractor submits that notice for you if you hire one. The call is free. Ask us to confirm when that fits into the schedule so utilities are marked before the ground is opened.

A camera versus a cable

Cable only

  • Clears a blockage
  • Does not show the cause
  • Quick and low cost
  • Clog may return

Camera inspection

  • Shows the pipe's condition
  • Finds roots, cracks and sags
  • Records evidence you can keep
  • Helps decide cleaning versus repair
