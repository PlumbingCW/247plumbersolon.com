Why jetting

When a cable only makes a hole

A snake pokes a channel through a clog. The water then drains for a while, but grease, scale and root fibers still coat the pipe wall, and the line narrows again. That is why a drain can run fine for two weeks and then back up on a holiday weekend.

Hydro jetting sends water through a hose with a nozzle that points backward and forward. The jets scrub the full circumference of the pipe and pull the hose along, flushing debris out ahead of it. It is the tool we reach for when the problem is buildup, not a single object. For one stubborn fixture, plain drain cleaning is usually enough.

Signs your line needs more than a cable

It clogs again within weeks

Fast refilling means the pipe wall is still coated. The channel a cable cut has closed back up.

A previous snake stalled partway

A cable that stops or jams somewhere along the run points to roots, a heavy grease mass or a pipe problem worth seeing on camera.

Slow drains in more than one room

Several slow fixtures share one line. The narrowing is downstream of all of them.

Gurgling after the washer drains

The line can no longer take a surge of water, which is how a partial blockage first shows itself.

Grease heavy kitchen or food business

Cooking grease coats pipes in layers. A jet is the right tool for a restaurant or busy home kitchen line.

Cable versus jet, side by side

A cable or auger

  • Good for a single clog in one fixture
  • Opens a channel through the blockage
  • Leaves a film on the pipe wall
  • Quick and lighter on equipment

Hydro jetting

  • Good for buildup along the whole run
  • Scours the wall and flushes debris out
  • Cuts small roots with the right nozzle
  • Needs a camera check first on older lines

What a jetting visit looks like

The visit follows one rule. Look first, then clean.

  1. 1

    Camera first

    We run a small camera when the pipe is old or a cable stalled. Pressure on a cracked or collapsed pipe can make it worse, so we want to see before we blast.

  2. 2

    Choose the nozzle

    A flat nozzle scours grease. A rotating one cuts roots. The choice depends on what the camera or the cable showed.

  3. 3

    Jet toward the cleanout

    We feed the hose in from the cleanout and work through the run, flushing debris to a spot we can capture and remove.

  4. 4

    Check the result

    We camera the line again when needed, run water, and show you the pipe wall so you see the difference.

Local note

Hard water and scale in Solon lines

Cleveland Water supplies Solon and states its water measures about 7 grains per gallon of hardness, which it classes as moderately hard. Hard water leaves mineral deposits on fixtures and in hot lines, and it makes soap film cling to drain walls.

That buildup is one reason an older line narrows even when no one has done anything wrong. If the same cleaning keeps failing in your home, tell us. It helps us pick a nozzle and judge whether the pipe wall is worth cleaning or worth a closer look.

Where a jet does not belong

Very old clay, orangeburg or cast iron lines can be fragile. High pressure inside a weak pipe can blow out a section, so we judge the pipe before we choose the method. Homes in Solon span many decades, so we do not assume one pipe type.

If your home has a bellied section where water pools, jetting will clear it but the pool returns. A camera inspection shows how deep it is and whether regular cleaning or a repair makes more sense.
