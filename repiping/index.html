When patching stops making sense

One leak is a repair. The third leak on the same line in a year is a pattern, and each splice leaves older pipe on either side of it. At that point you are paying for the same visit over and over while the rest of the run keeps thinning.

We look at three things to decide: how many failures the line has had, what the pipe looks like where we cut, and how the water behaves at your fixtures. If a spot repair is enough, we say so. Selling you a whole-house job you do not need is not how we want your call to go.

What worn pipes tell you before they fail

Brown or orange water after it sits

Rust is coming off the pipe wall. It tends to show first at the tap farthest from the main.

Pinhole leaks that keep coming back

Mineral stains or a damp spot on a pipe that you already patched mean the wall is thin along the whole run.

Weak flow at several fixtures

When pressure drops in the shower and the kitchen, buildup inside the pipe may be narrowing the passage.

Green crust at copper joints

That is corrosion working on the metal and the solder, and it often precedes a drip.

A quiet leak you cannot find

A meter that moves with every tap off may need leak detection first so we know whether one run or many are at fault.

How old Solon houses shape the pipe question

The ACS profile of Solon, built from 2012 to 2016 data, puts the median home at 1981. About 31 percent of the housing was built from 1950 to 1979, and roughly 4 percent before 1940. Older homes like these often still carry their original supply pipe or early repairs, so the age of your house is a fair clue about what we will find.

That is a guide, not a measurement. Plenty of homes were repiped after construction, so we check what is actually in your walls and basement before we give a plan. Tell us the year the house was built when you call, and we will bring the right fittings.

PEX or copper, what each means for your house

PEX

  • Flexible tubing that bends around corners, so fewer joints sit inside walls
  • Usually quicker to install and less cutting of drywall
  • Cannot be left in direct sunlight and needs support at set spacing
  • Does not corrode or scale the way metal does

Copper

  • Rigid metal with a long track record in Ohio homes
  • Soldered joints take more time and need careful fire safety
  • Can pit or corrode where water chemistry is harsh
  • Easy to match when only part of a house is being replaced

What a repipe touches and what it leaves alone

Repiping covers the supply lines inside your house, from where water enters to the fixtures. Cleveland Water's standards put the city-owned line between the main and the curb stop, and everything from the back of the curb stop to the house with you. We can replace your side when it is the weak link.

Lead service lines are a separate question. Cleveland Water publishes a service line inventory at clevelandwater.com/line, and its FAQ says homes built after 1953 are unlikely to have one. The city-owned section is Cleveland Water's job. If you are curious about your own side, check the inventory and tell us what you find.
