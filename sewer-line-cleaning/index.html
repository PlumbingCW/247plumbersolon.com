How you know it is the main line and not one drain

One slow sink is a fixture problem. These patterns point to the line that leaves the house.

Floor drain bubbles or rises

The basement floor drain is the lowest opening in the house, so it shows a blocked main first.

Toilet flushes and the tub gurgles

Water has to push past a blockage downstream, and air escapes through the nearest trap.

Washing machine drains and a basement fixture overflows

A sudden surge exposes how little room is left in the pipe.

Several drains slow at once

When upstairs and downstairs fixtures all lag together, the shared line is the likely culprit.

Sewer smell in the yard or basement

A persistent odor can mean a partial blockage, a dry trap or a damaged section. We look to see which.

Where the blockage usually sits and what causes it

Most main line stoppages come from tree roots that enter at joints, grease that hardens on the pipe wall, or flushed items that snag on a rough spot. Older clay and cast iron pipe have more joints and more rough surfaces to catch debris.

The cure depends on the cause. Soft buildup comes out with a cable and a cutting head. Heavy grease or repeat roots call for hydro jetting, which scours the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through it. We tell you which one fits before work starts.

Local note

Who owns which section of the line in Solon

The City of Solon says the property owner is responsible for the lateral from the house to the cleanout. The city maintains its own sewer main in the street, and its Service Department asks residents with sewage backing up into a basement to call it. Crews check the main first, and if it is clear they point you to a licensed plumber.

Make that call while we are on the way if you can. It rules out a city main problem, and it keeps you from paying for work that would not have fixed a street side blockage. The city has not published exactly where the cleanout boundary sits, so we look at your layout together.

From the cleanout to a clear line

  1. 1

    Open the cleanout carefully

    Backed up water can shoot out when the cap loosens, so we release it slowly and control the flow.

  2. 2

    Run a cable to the stoppage

    A larger drum machine reaches farther than a household snake. We cut through roots or grease until the cable moves freely.

  3. 3

    Flush and test

    We run water through the line and check that it flows without hesitation from every fixture.

  4. 4

    Show you what we found

    If the cable pulled up roots or pipe scale, we tell you. A camera inspection can follow when the cause is unclear.

Why a foundation drain should never reach this line

Solon's code states that foundation drains may not be tied into the sanitary sewer, and that the sanitary sewer is not meant to carry storm or ground water. When clean rain water is forced into a sewer lateral, it fills the pipe during heavy storms and can push sewage back into a home.

If your lateral backs up mostly after hard rain, tell us. We cannot tell you how your house is plumbed from here, and the city building department is the right place to ask about connections.
