How to Install a Toilet in Solon, OH, Step by Step

Swapping a toilet is a two to three hour job for a careful homeowner: shut off the water, remove the old bowl, check the flange, set a new wax ring, and bolt the new toilet down. The step that goes wrong most often is the flange, not the toilet.

Here is the full sequence, the measurement to take before you shop, and what Solon's permit guide says about fixture replacement.

Measure before you shop

Measure from the wall behind the toilet to the center of the floor bolts. Most toilets are built for a 12 inch rough-in, but older homes sometimes have 10 or 14 inch spacing. A toilet made for the wrong measurement will not sit flush against the wall.

Also check the shape of the bowl. Round bowls take less room than elongated ones, and the difference can be an inch or two. Measure from the same bolts to the front of the old bowl, then compare with the box dimensions.

Finally, decide on height. Taller bowls are easier on knees, but they may look out of place beside an older vanity. Sit on one in the store if you can.

Any row ending in stop and call is a signal to pause. Setting a new toilet over rotten wood only hides the problem.

Removing the old toilet

Close the supply valve behind the toilet and flush until the tank empties. Hold the handle down to get the last of the water out. Then use a sponge to clear what remains in the tank and the bowl.

Disconnect the supply line, remove the nuts on the closet bolts, and rock the toilet gently to break the wax seal. Lift straight up with a helper if the bowl is heavy, then set it on cardboard or an old towel.

Stuff a rag in the open drain pipe right away. Sewer gas is unpleasant and unhealthy, and dropped tools or old wax can fall down the pipe.

Setting the new toilet

Work in this order and the new bowl should seat without a wobble.

  1. 1

    Clean the flange

    Scrape off the old wax with a putty knife and wipe the ring dry. Check for cracks, rust, and a flange height that sits level with or slightly above the finished floor.

  2. 2

    Place the new bolts and wax ring

    Slide new closet bolts into the flange slots. Set the wax ring on the flange or the bowl horn, but never both.

  3. 3

    Lower the bowl straight down

    Line up the bolts with the holes and press the bowl down firmly. Do not twist it back and forth, which breaks the wax seal.

  4. 4

    Tighten in small turns

    Alternate the nuts, a quarter turn at a time. Overtightening cracks the porcelain, which is a costly mistake.

  5. 5

    Mount the tank, connect water, and test

    Bolt the tank to the bowl, attach the supply line, and open the valve slowly. Flush several times and check the base and connections for drips.

Local note

Permits, water, and state code for a Solon bathroom

Solon's Required Residential Permits Guide lists new plumbing installations, extending or running a new plumbing line, and replacing a water heater as permit items. It does not require a permit for replacing fixtures. A like-for-like toilet swap falls on the fixture side.

Moving the toilet to a new spot, tapping into a new drain, or running a new supply line is a different job. Call the Solon Building Department before you start. The city has adopted the Ohio Plumbing Code, which applies to one-, two-, and three-family homes.

Cleveland Water states that Solon's supply measures 7 grains per gallon, or 120 mg/L. Mineral scale can crust the fill valve and the supply connection over time. Rinse the new valve seat whenever you service it.

When a plumber is the better choice

If the flange is broken, the floor is soft, or the drain pipe sits at an odd angle, a repair stops being a swap. These jobs involve cutting into floors and sometimes the drain line itself. Mistakes can leak into the ceiling below.

Weight is a real factor too. A one-piece toilet can weigh around 100 pounds. If you cannot lift it safely, do not try.

For a stubborn running or leaking toilet, toilet repair is a better starting point than a new bowl. For a new bathroom or a moved fixture, plumbing installation covers the supply and drain work.

What changes the cost of a toilet install

The toilet itself is the biggest line item. Basic two-piece bowls cost far less than comfort-height or one-piece designs. The plumber's labor depends on how easily the old toilet comes out and what shape the flange is in.

Flange repair, a new shutoff valve, new supply lines, and subfloor patching all add cost. Haul-away of the old toilet is another small item. Ask for these on the quote so nothing surprises you.

Finishing touches that prevent callbacks

Caulk around the base after the toilet is tight, but leave a small gap at the back so a hidden leak has somewhere to show itself. Wipe away any extra caulk with a damp cloth.

Check the fill level and the flush after a day. A fill valve that hisses, or a flapper that lets water trickle into the bowl, is much easier to adjust now than after a month of wasted water.
