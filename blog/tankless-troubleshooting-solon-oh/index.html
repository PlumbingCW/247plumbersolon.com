Tankless Water Heater Troubleshooting in Solon, OH: Fixes You Can Try First

A tankless water heater that runs cold, flashes a code, or shuts off mid-shower is usually asking for one of a few things. This guide covers the checks that are safe for a homeowner and the signs that mean it is time for a plumber.

Start with what the heater shows you

Most tankless units have a display that shows a numbered code or a flashing light pattern. Write down the exact code before you do anything else, and take a photo of the screen. Codes point toward ignition, venting, flow, or sensor trouble, which narrows the cause quickly.

Your manual lists what each code means, but codes differ between manufacturers, so a code from another brand's chart can mislead you. If you cannot find the manual, read the model number on the data plate and search the manufacturer's site for the guide.

Five checks any homeowner can do

  • Power.. Confirm the outlet or breaker is on and the unit is plugged in or switched on.
  • Gas valve or breaker feeding an electric unit.. Make sure the valve handle runs in line with the pipe. An electric unit may need a large dedicated breaker.
  • Water shutoffs.. Check the inlet and outlet valves are fully open. A half-closed valve is a common cause of low flow.
  • Inlet screen.. Close the isolation valves, release pressure, and rinse the small filter screen if your manual shows one.
  • Faucet aerators.. Remove and rinse the aerator on the fixture that runs cold. Debris there can trip the flow sensor.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not troubleshoot a gas appliance with a suspected leak.

The cold water sandwich and low flow

Many people notice a burst of cold water between two hot ones. That happens when you shut the tap briefly, the heater turns off, and hot water in the pipes cools before you turn it on again. Running the water longer, or opening the tap slightly more, helps the unit stay lit.

Low flow is a different issue. A tankless heater only fires when water moves past a minimum rate, so a clogged aerator, a partly closed valve, or scale in the lines can keep it from turning on. If flushing the screen and the aerator does not help, flow is probably the cause.

Local note

What Solon water does inside a tankless heater

Cleveland Water, which supplies Solon, states its water hardness is 7 grains per gallon or 120 mg/L as calcium carbonate. The 2025 water quality report lists hardness between 105 and 130 ppm with an average of 117. That is moderately hard water, which leaves mineral scale on heat exchangers over time.

Scale is the reason many tankless units here need periodic flushing. We cannot tell you how fast it builds in your house, since that depends on use and temperature, so check the manufacturer's flush interval and test your own tap. If your heater is losing output or banging, descaling is the first thing to rule out.

Flushing the unit: what to expect

A flush circulates a descaling solution or white vinegar through the heat exchanger using a small pump and two hoses connected to service valves. Your unit needs isolation valves installed for this to work well, and not every older installation has them.

Do not run strong acid through the unit unless the manufacturer allows it. If the service valves are missing or stuck, a plumber can add them first. Our tankless water heater service covers flushing, valve installation, and replacement when the heat exchanger has failed.

Venting, gas, and condensate trouble

Gas tankless units need clear exhaust and combustion air. A blocked vent, a bird nest, or snow against a sidewall termination can lock the unit out. High-efficiency models also drain acidic condensate, and a plugged condensate line or trap can trigger a fault.

Never alter a vent pipe yourself, and never run a gas heater with the vent disconnected. Carbon monoxide has no smell, so test your CO alarms. If an alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911 from outside.

Permits, repair, and replacement

Solon's Required Residential Permits Guide says replacing a water heater needs a plumbing permit. A repair of a part usually does not, but a replacement does, so ask the Solon Building Department if you are unsure. A licensed plumber who works in the city should know the process.

Replace the unit when the heat exchanger leaks, repeated codes return after repairs, or the model is old and parts are scarce. If the problem is a part, our water heater repair visit can often handle it. Either way, you get a clear price before work starts.

What affects the price of a tankless fix

Price follows the part, the access, and the fuel. A sensor or igniter is a different job than a heat exchanger, and a vent repair adds time. After-hours calls and hard-to-reach units raise labor. Descaling is usually a flat-rate task, but missing service valves add parts.
