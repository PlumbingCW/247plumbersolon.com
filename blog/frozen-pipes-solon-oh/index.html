Frozen Pipes in Solon, OH: What To Do Before They Burst

If a faucet runs as a trickle or not at all during a hard freeze, a pipe is probably frozen. Open the tap, shut off the main valve if you see any water, and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer. Never use an open flame.

Below are the signs, a safe thaw, what Solon's winters have done to local water systems, and the point where you should stop and call a plumber.

How to tell a pipe is frozen

The first sign is usually a faucet that gives you a weak stream or nothing at all while others still work. Frost on an exposed pipe, a bulge in the line, or a metallic smell from the tap are other hints. If only the hot or only the cold side is dead, the frozen section is on that line.

Frozen pipes usually sit where cold air reaches them. Look along exterior walls, in unheated garages, crawl spaces, and near the rim joists in a basement. Pipes feeding outdoor spigots are common trouble spots too.

What to do the moment water stops

Do these in order, and keep the heat on in the house.

  • Open the affected tap.. Leave it open so melting ice and trapped pressure have somewhere to go.
  • Find the main shutoff.. Cleveland Water notes that the master shutoff is typically where the water line enters the basement or crawl space from the street. Know where it is before you need it.
  • Look for water first.. If you see a wet ceiling, a spray, or a puddle near the pipe, close the main valve and call. A frozen pipe that has already cracked will flood when it thaws.
  • Warm the room.. Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and raise the thermostat a few degrees.
  • Apply gentle heat.. Use a hair dryer, a heating pad, or warm towels, starting near the tap and working toward the frozen spot.

What never to use on a frozen pipe

Skip torches, heat guns, and kerosene heaters. Fast heat can crack a pipe, scorch framing, or ignite insulation. Even a propane torch on copper can start a fire inside a wall.

Space heaters work if you set them on a hard floor and keep them well away from anything flammable. Never leave one running unattended. A hair dryer is slower, but it is far safer.

Do not use salt, antifreeze, or chemicals in a drinking water line. They are not made for it and can contaminate the supply.

Local note

What Northeast Ohio winters have done to local water lines

Cleveland Water, which supplies Solon, says the frost depth in northern Ohio is 3 to 3 1/2 feet, and its mains are buried more than 5 feet deep. Your service line from the street is shallower than the main, and your indoor pipes are not buried at all. That is why freezing trouble shows up inside homes first.

In the first five days of January 2018, Cleveland Water reported over 100 active main breaks in its system. February 2015 was Cleveland's coldest February on record, with an average of 14.3 degrees and a low of minus 17 on the 20th, according to the National Weather Service. Both are reminders that long cold snaps stress buried and indoor pipes alike.

Cleveland Water's curb stop marks the line between city and owner. The owner is responsible for the pipe from the curb stop to the house. If a frozen service line is the problem, call a plumber to find out which side of the stop it is on.

Where pipes freeze first in a Solon house

Garages and exterior kitchen walls top the list. A sink on an outside wall has supply lines running through the coldest space in the house. Cabinet doors closed on that wall keep warm air out.

Basements are not safe by default. Pipes along rim joists, near window wells, or beside a leaky sill plate see drafts all winter. Unfinished crawl spaces and utility rooms carry the same risk.

Outdoor hose bibs deserve their own mention. A hose left attached can trap water inside the spigot and split the pipe behind the wall. Disconnect hoses in the fall and shut off any interior valve to the spigot if you have one.

Prevention that costs very little

  • Disconnect garden hoses and cover or shut off exterior spigots before the first hard freeze.
  • Wrap exposed pipes in foam sleeves, especially in garages, crawl spaces, and along exterior walls.
  • Seal drafts around pipe entry points with caulk or spray foam.
  • Let a faucet drip during a deep freeze. Moving water is slower to freeze, and the drip relieves pressure.
  • Keep the thermostat at a steady temperature if you travel, and ask a neighbor to check the house.

None of these is a promise against freezing. A very long cold snap can beat every precaution, so know where your main shutoff is.

What shapes the cost when a pipe does burst

The price depends on how much pipe is damaged, how hard it is to reach, and what material it is made from. A split in an open basement run is a short job. A burst behind a finished wall means opening drywall and repairing it afterward.

Water damage cleanup is usually a separate matter from the plumbing repair. Homeowners policies usually cover sudden, accidental water damage but not wear or neglect, so check yours and take photos before cleanup.

When to call a plumber, and who to call

If you can reach the frozen section and there is no sign of damage, a careful thaw is reasonable. If you see damage, cannot find the freeze, or the whole house is without water, call for burst pipe repair. If a freeze keeps recurring in the same spot, repiping the exposed run may be the real fix.
