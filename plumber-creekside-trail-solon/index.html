What is documented

The Creekside Trail drainage study

The City of Solon hired Burgess and Niple to study the flooding and develop an improvement plan for 21 residences. The firm's project page says it surveyed residents with a questionnaire, that 70 percent responded, and that it modeled the culvert and the storm sewer. The chosen fix was to lower the storm sewer profile.

The page adds that the required depth conflicted with an existing sanitary sewer, so the storm sewer was set slightly lower than the sanitary one. The firm says this should reduce the frequency and severity of basement backups. It does not give the year.

Separately, a public construction listing shows a City of Solon contract named Creekside Trail Storm Sewer Replacement and Windy Hill Sidewalk Installation, with bidding in autumn 2023. We cannot say whether it is the same work as the study. Ask the city for the current status.

Two kinds of water

Storm water and sewage are different problems

In Solon's code, the sanitary sewer is not meant to carry storm, surface or ground water. Knowing which kind you have decides who to call.

Rain or groundwater

  • Seeps in at the wall or floor edge after heavy rain
  • Clear or muddy, no sewage smell
  • Sump pit fills or runs without stopping
  • Point your downspouts away from the house and test the pump

Sewage backup

  • Floor drain or tub bubbles, several fixtures slow
  • Dirty water with a sewage smell
  • Starts without much rain
  • Stop using water and call the city Service Department

What to do while the storm is on

  • ✓Check the pump. Lift the float with a stick. The pump should start and discharge outdoors.
  • ✓Move what you can. Lift boxes and electronics off the floor.
  • ✓Photograph the water. Photos show the level and the source for your records.
  • ✓Stay out of standing water. If it reaches outlets or the panel, leave the area.
  • ✓Do not run the washer or dishwasher. In a backup, that water goes down the same lateral.
Local note

Backwater valves and sewer work need the city

A backwater valve is a device some homeowners add to a lateral to slow a backup. We could not find a Solon rule that requires or bans one, so ask before buying. The city's permit guide lists utility installations including sewers and drains, so a lateral change may need a permit.

Digging calls for 811 at least two working days ahead. Mark where your clean out is before the crew comes.
