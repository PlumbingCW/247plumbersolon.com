Find the water's path before choosing a fix

Basement water arrives by a few routes. Rain runs along the foundation wall and seeps through a crack or the joint where wall meets floor. Groundwater rises under the slab. Gutters dump roof water at the corner. Each route calls for a different solution, so the first job is deciding which one you actually have.

A simple test helps. Tape a square of plastic wrap to a damp wall for a day or two. Moisture on the outside of the plastic is humid air, and moisture under it is water moving through the wall. That one check can keep you from paying for the wrong repair.

What a leaking foundation tends to look like

White crust on the wall

Mineral salts left behind as moisture passes through block or concrete and dries at the surface.

Damp edge where wall meets floor

This joint is a common entry point because water collects at the lowest seam.

Musty smell without visible water

Humidity from a damp wall or slab, often worst after several wet days.

Peeling paint or bubbling sealer

Water pushing out from behind the coating, so more paint will not solve it.

Horizontal crack in a block wall

A sign of soil pressure against the wall. This is a structural question for an engineer, not a plumbing patch.

What a plumber handles and what needs someone else

Plumbing-side fixes

  • Interior perimeter drain tied to a sump pit
  • Sump pump sizing, install and discharge line
  • Check valve and discharge routing
  • Leaking supply or drain pipes mistaken for seepage
  • Floor drain and backwater problems

Outside our scope

  • Structural cracks or bowing walls
  • Regrading the yard
  • Exterior excavation and membrane work
  • New gutters and roof drainage design
  • Foundation underpinning

Local note

Solon rules that shape a basement drain system

Solon's sewer code states that no foundation drains shall be tied into the sanitary sewer, and that sanitary sewers are not meant to carry storm, surface or ground water. The city also dye tests downspouts, driveways and area drains for rain water leaking into the sanitary system. A perimeter drain has to end at a sump pump and a proper outside discharge.

The city's stormwater page encourages disconnecting downspouts and using rain barrels, and some of that work may need a permit. Check with the Solon engineering office before you move a downspout, because routing roof water away from the house is often the cheapest part of any dry-basement plan.
