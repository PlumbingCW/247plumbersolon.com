Why on-demand hot water appeals, and where it bites

A tankless heater warms water as it flows, so there is no tank to run out. That saves floor space and ends the wait for a recovering tank. It also shifts the workload to a compact heat exchanger that does a lot in a small space.

That compact core is the weak point. Narrow passages collect scale, and a heavily scaled unit throws error codes or loses flow. A tankless heater works best when it is sized right, vented right, and flushed on a schedule.

Local note

Lake Erie water leaves scale behind

Cleveland Water, which supplies Solon, describes its water as about 7 grains per gallon, or roughly 120 milligrams per liter, and calls that moderately hard. Those minerals settle inside a heat exchanger as the water heats. That makes a scheduled flush a practical habit here, not a luxury.

Check your manufacturer's instructions for the flush interval. If you run a softener, mention it when you call, since it changes how we set up and judge the unit.

What a flush and descale visit covers

  1. 1

    Isolate the unit

    We shut off gas or power and close the water valves so the unit can be serviced safely.

  2. 2

    Circulate descaler

    A pump pushes a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for a set time to dissolve scale.

  3. 3

    Rinse and clean screens

    We flush out the solution and clean the inlet filter screen, where debris tends to collect.

  4. 4

    Check codes and venting

    We review error history, test the flame or elements, and look over the flue for damage.

Signs your unit is asking for service

If you ever smell gas near the unit, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us once everyone is safe.

Error code on the display

Codes point toward flame, venting, flow, or sensor problems. Write it down before you call.

Hot water that goes lukewarm

Scale or a partly blocked screen can limit how hot the unit can make the water.

Weak flow at the tap

A clogged inlet screen or scaled exchanger shows up as lower flow before total failure.

Long wait for hot water

A unit that fires late or shuts off early may have a sensor or flow issue.

Unusual noises or cycling

Banging, clicking, or on-off cycling often traces back to scale or restricted venting.

Tankless has a ceiling

Every on-demand unit has a maximum flow it can heat. Two showers and a dishwasher at once may exceed it, and the result is water that turns cooler. Sizing the unit to your busiest morning avoids that. Ask about a larger model or a second unit if your household is big.
