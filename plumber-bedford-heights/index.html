Basement drain backing up

Water rising in a floor drain or tub usually means the main line, not one fixture. Stop running water and call.

Water heater leaking or cold

A tank dripping from the base is done. A tank with no heat may only need a part, and we check before we recommend a swap.

Pipe leaking inside a wall

A stained ceiling or a warm spot on the floor points to a supply line. Shut off the main and leave the wall alone until we look.

Sump pump silent in a storm

A pump that hums but moves nothing has a stuck float or a failed motor. Either way the basement is on a clock.

Frozen pipe after a cold snap

No water at one tap in January often means ice in an outside wall. Do not use a torch, and call before it splits.

What a 1960s Bedford Heights house usually needs

An older census profile puts the median home in Bedford Heights at 1967, and about 40 percent of the housing was built in the 1960s alone. Another 18 percent came in the 1950s and 19 percent in the 1970s. That is a lot of houses now past 50 years old.

Homes from this era often have original shutoff valves that stick or weep when turned, supply lines that have hardened, and water heaters that have been replaced at least once. We carry that in mind when you call: if your valve will not close, we bring a replacement rather than force it and break it.

About half the units in the city are owner-occupied, so we also hear from renters and landlords. Tell us who owns the unit when you call, since that decides who approves the repair and who gets the invoice.

Drain backup: is it the city's line or yours?

Bedford Heights runs its own Water Reclamation Department and the Robert Williford Wastewater Plant on Solon Road. The city says its crews respond to completely blocked drains and that residents can call the department for help with the line from the house to the street. The city posts separate daytime and evening numbers on its wastewater page.

Here is how that helps you. Run a faucet upstairs and flush a toilet. If only one drain is slow, the clog is inside your house and we can usually clear it with a cable. If every drain gurgles at once, call the city and call us, because a sewer line cleaning visit can show whether the blockage sits in your pipe or past it.

Exactly where the city's help ends is not spelled out on its page, so ask the department what it covers at your address. We will camera the line if the blockage keeps coming back, and show you the footage.

Before we arrive

While a plumber is headed to your Bedford Heights address

1Find the main shutoff

It is usually where the water line enters the basement or crawl space. Turn it clockwise and leave it off until we look.

2Stay away from standing water

If water is near the panel, outlets, or a running sump, keep out. Do not touch anything electrical while you are wet.

3Stop using the drains

Skip flushing, laundry, and the dishwasher during a backup. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go but the floor.

4Take photos for your insurer

Photograph the water, the fixture, and any damage before moving things. Then lift valuables and rugs off the floor.
