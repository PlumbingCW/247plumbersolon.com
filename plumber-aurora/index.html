Water source

Two water systems share one city name

Aurora's Water Department runs two systems. The Portage system pulls groundwater from the Coit Road well field and softens it with ion exchange at a treatment plant in Shalersville. The Cleveland system delivers Lake Erie water from Cleveland Water.

The city's reports do not say which streets get which supply, so ask the water department which one serves your address. It matters when a heater fails and you shop for water heater replacement. Softened water is gentle on a new tank, while Cleveland-system water carries moderate hardness near 7 grains per gallon, and scale builds faster on elements and fixtures.

Four kinds of Aurora homes and what tends to give out

Dispatch is open around the clock, so none of these has to wait for business hours. The age of the house points to the likeliest culprit.

1920s houses in the historic center

The city's commuter-village plan produced homes along Hurd Road, Shawnee Trail, Hudson Road and Pioneer Trail by 1929. Homes this old often still have galvanized or early copper lines, which narrow and leak at joints.

Aurora Shores lakeside homes

Building began there in late 1971, and about 887 homes surround a 580-acre lake. At roughly fifty years old, original heaters and sump pumps are well past their expected life.

The 1990s subdivisions

About 27 percent of Aurora's housing was built in that decade, according to survey data built on Census figures. Expect first or second heaters, worn faucet cartridges and disposals.

New townhomes at the Geauga Lake site

Renaissance Park is recent construction, so failures are usually fixtures, supply stops and drain traps rather than pipe age.

Sump pumps near the lake and the Chagrin branch

The city's stormwater guide tells residents to have their sump pumps inspected regularly and to check storm laterals for roots and debris. That is sound advice, because a pump you only test during a storm is a pump that fails during a storm. A yearly look at the float and the check valve costs little next to a flooded finished basement.

If the pit keeps filling even with a healthy pump, the problem may be groundwater or a clogged storm lateral rather than the pump. Our sump pump repair crew can sort that out, and basement waterproofing is the next step when repairs only buy time.

Local note

Permits: what Aurora's own sheet lists

Aurora's Planning, Zoning and Building Division publishes a residential permit sheet that lists plumbing gas lines, miscellaneous plumbing, waterproofing and foundation repair, and replacement water heaters. So a heater swap or a basement waterproofing job is not something to do on the quiet.

Portage County's building department says it does not serve Aurora and does not handle plumbing, and the county health department handles plumbing countywide. How that splits inside Aurora is not spelled out publicly, so call the city division before booking and ask who inspects. We can work around whatever answer you get.

While you wait

Steps to take in the first few minutes

1Shut off the main

Turn the main valve clockwise. If you use a private well or softener loop, close that too.

2Cut power in the wet zone

Do not stand in water near a panel or outlet. If you cannot reach the breaker dry, call the electric utility.

3Keep drains quiet

Skip the dishwasher, washer and showers if a drain is backing up. Extra water has nowhere to go.

4Document the damage

Take photos before anything is moved. Your homeowners insurer will want them.
