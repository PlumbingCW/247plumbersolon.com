A faucet or valve that will not shut off

Find the oval or round handle on the supply line under the sink and turn it clockwise. If it spins without stopping the water or the handle is crusted solid, go to the main shutoff, usually where the water line enters your basement. Cleveland Water notes that this valve is typically found where the line comes in from the street.

Once the flow stops, open the faucet to drain what is left in the line and put a bucket under any dripping joint. Photos of the leak help us bring the right cartridge, seat, or valve. If water is coming through a ceiling or wall, leave the main off and tell the dispatcher right away.

What is usually behind a leaking fixture

A worn cartridge or washer

This is the classic drip at the spout. The part wears from use and mineral scale, and replacing it often restores the fixture.

Cracked or dried O-rings

Leaks around the handle or at the base of a swivel spout come from these small rings. They cost little and take little time.

Pitted valve seats

Scale eats the seat that the washer presses against. A seat that is badly pitted can keep a faucet dripping after a new washer.

A failing supply line

Braided lines age and kink. A line that sweats, bulges, or rusts at the fitting should be replaced, not tightened again.

A stuck or leaking stop valve

Old shutoffs corrode and then will not close when you need them most. Replacing one now saves a mess later.

Tubs, showers, and outdoor spigots

Bathtub and shower valves hide behind tile, so a leak there can run for weeks before a stain shows up. Water on the ceiling below or soft drywall near the tub is a reason to call the same day. If the drip persists after we replace a cartridge, we move to leak detection to find where the water is really traveling.

Outdoor hose bibs are another winter risk. Northern Ohio frost reaches roughly 3 to 3.5 feet, according to Cleveland Water, and a hose left attached can freeze the pipe behind the spigot. If a spigot sprays inside the wall after a thaw, shut the main and call, because that can quickly become burst pipe repair.

Local note

Hard water and permits on Solon fixtures

Cleveland Water, which serves Solon, says its water is about 7 grains per gallon, roughly 120 mg/L as calcium carbonate. Scale builds in aerators, cartridges, and valve seats, so a faucet in Solon can lose pressure long before it leaks. Test your tap or ask your water utility if you are weighing a softener.

The city's Required Residential Permits Guide says replacing fixtures needs no permit, but new plumbing lines do. If a faucet swap turns into moving a sink, that becomes plumbing installation work, so call the Solon Building Department first.
