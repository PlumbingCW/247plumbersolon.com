Standing water in the house: do this before we arrive

A backed up drain gets worse every time someone uses water. A few minutes of prep keeps the mess small and lets us start work the moment we walk in.

  • ✓Stop all water use.No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher. Every gallon you send down has nowhere to go but back up.
  • ✓Note which drains are affected.One slow sink points to a branch line. A tub gurgling when the toilet flushes points to the main.
  • ✓Keep people and pets out of the wet area.Backed up water from a floor drain or toilet can carry bacteria, so wear boots and gloves if you must walk through it.
  • ✓Skip the chemical drain liquid.It sits in standing water, burns skin, and makes the job unsafe for whoever reaches into the trap next.
  • ✓Clear a path to the cleanout.If you know where the basement cleanout cap is, move boxes away from it so we can get a cable in quickly.

Which clue points to which clog

Most callers describe a symptom, not a pipe. These are the patterns we read first.

One sink drains slowly

The clog is usually in that fixture's own trap or branch line. Hair, grease and soap film are the usual causes.

Tub gurgles when the toilet flushes

Air and water are fighting through a partly blocked line shared by both. That pattern often means the main drain.

Floor drain smells or bubbles

The basement floor drain sits at the lowest point. It shows trouble first when the main line slows down.

Clog comes back every few weeks

A line that clears and refills fast usually has roots, scale or a belly holding debris. Cutting the clog is only half the fix.

Everything drains, but slowly

That points downstream of every fixture, in the main line or the lateral to the street side of the house.

Cable, auger or water jet: how we choose

A kitchen sink with a grease plug does not need the same tool as a four-inch main full of roots. We start with the lightest tool that will do the job, then step up if the cable comes back with nothing on it.

A hand auger or a drum cable handles most single-fixture clogs. For long runs, heavy buildup or roots, a cutting head on a larger cable or a pressure jet scours the pipe wall instead of just poking a hole through the middle. If a cable stalls partway, that is a signal. Something solid may be in the line, and a camera look is the next step, covered on our sewer camera inspection page.

Local note

Whose pipe is clogged: yours or the city's

In Solon, the city says the property owner is responsible for the sewer lateral from the house to the cleanout. The city maintains its own sewer main in the street. If sewage is backing up into a basement, the city asks residents to call its Service Department, and crews check the main first.

That order saves you money. If the main is clear, the city recommends a licensed plumber, and that is where we come in. Telling the Service Department about the backup first can rule out a street-side problem before you pay for a cleaning that cannot fix it.

Kitchen sinks, laundry lines and shower drains

Kitchen lines clog with grease that cools and hardens on the pipe wall, so a sink that drains slowly after dishes is warning you early. Laundry standpipes clog with lint. Shower drains clog with hair wrapped around soap scum, and the plug usually sits a few inches below the strainer.

These jobs are quick when the blockage is local. If a second fixture starts to slow down after we clear the first, we stop treating it as a one-sink problem and look at the shared line behind it.
