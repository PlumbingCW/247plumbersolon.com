The first two minutes decide how bad the floor gets

  • ✓Close the main valve.Cleveland Water says the master shutoff usually sits where the line enters the basement or crawl space. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • ✓Drain what is left.Open the lowest cold tap and a few upstairs taps. Less water behind the break means less on your floor.
  • ✓Protect the electrics.If water drips near a panel, outlet or light fixture, stay clear. Cut the breaker only if you can reach it from dry ground.
  • ✓Photograph before you mop.Take wide and close photos of the break and the wet areas. Your insurer will want them, and they help us plan the repair.
  • ✓Turn off the water heater.A burst on the hot side can keep draining the tank. Shut the heater down so it does not run dry and damage itself, and we can look at it during water heater repair if it was hit.

If you smell gas at any point, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call anyone else. Then call (440) 468-0237 and tell dispatch where the water is coming from.

Local note

Why a deep freeze is hard on Cuyahoga County pipes

Cleveland Water reports a frost depth of 3 to 3.5 feet in northern Ohio and buries its mains more than five feet down. The pipes in your exterior walls, garage ceiling or crawl space get no such cover. If you have a run on an outside wall, check it first after a freeze.

Cleveland Water logged over 100 active main breaks in the first five days of January 2018, and February 2015 was Cleveland's coldest February on record at a 14.3 degree average. Ice can split a pipe long before anything leaks. The water often appears when the thaw arrives, so look for it as temperatures climb.

Five ways a pipe fails besides ice

Freezing gets the blame, but it is not the only cause. The cause tells us whether to fix one spot or look at the whole run, and a slow loss that came first may call for leak detection as well.

Corrosion and age

Metal thins from the inside, often at threaded joints, until one weak spot gives way.

Pressure spikes

A slamming valve or a pressure surge sends a shock through the line and cracks an already tired section.

Failed fittings

A loose coupling or a cracked plastic elbow can let go even when the pipe itself is sound.

Damage during remodeling

A screw, nail or drill bit can nick a pipe that holds for months before it splits.

Shifting and settling

Slow movement in framing or soil strains rigid pipe at the points where it is anchored.

What happens once the crew is at your door

  1. 1

    Confirm the water is off

    We check your main, shut any other valves we need and relieve the pressure left in the lines.

  2. 2

    Find the failed section

    We trace the split and check the pipe on either side, because a break often has weak neighbors.

  3. 3

    Cut out and replace

    The bad stretch comes out and new pipe and fittings go in. If the fix grows into running new line, Solon's permit guide lists that as permit work, and we will say so up front.

  4. 4

    Restore and test

    We bring pressure back slowly, watch every joint and walk you through what we did before we leave.
