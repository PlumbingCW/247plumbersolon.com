Signs the water is escaping where you cannot see it

Meter dial moving with everything off

Every tap, appliance and ice maker is closed and the dial still creeps. Water is leaving the system somewhere past the meter.

A bill that jumped for no reason

Nobody added a guest or ran a hose, yet the number climbed. Cleveland Water bills quarterly, so a small steady leak can run for weeks before the bill shows it.

A warm patch on the floor

Warmth under tile or carpet often means a hot line is leaking beneath it. A faint hiss behind a wall points the same way.

Musty smell or bubbling paint

Moisture has been traveling for a while. Water follows framing, so the stain can sit several feet from the real source.

Weak pressure at every fixture

When the whole house loses pressure at once, suspect the supply line before you blame one faucet.

A meter that keeps spinning after every tap is off

Find your water meter. Cleveland Water's connection standards call for it within 36 inches of the basement floor, so look low on the wall where the line comes in. Shut off every faucet and appliance that uses water, then watch the small dial or triangle. Movement means water is escaping on the house side of the meter.

Write down the reading, wait an hour with nothing running, and read it again. A change tells us the leak is real and helps us decide whether to start at the meter or inside the house. It is a two-minute check that can catch a problem months before a quarterly bill would. If the pipe has already split, our burst pipe repair crew takes over.

How we hunt a leak without opening every wall

  1. 1

    Isolate the system

    We close sections one at a time and watch the meter. That tells us whether the loss sits on the service line, in the supply piping or behind a single fixture.

  2. 2

    Listen and scan

    Acoustic gear picks up the sound of escaping water through pipe and floor. A thermal camera shows cold or warm spots that dry surfaces hide.

  3. 3

    Pressure test the suspect run

    We cap the section and hold pressure. A line that will not hold narrows the search to one stretch of pipe.

  4. 4

    Open the smallest access we can

    Once the spot is pinned down, we cut only there. You get the repair plan and the price before any tool touches your wall.

Local note

Whose pipe is leaking in your yard

Cleveland Water's connection standards say the city-owned part of the service line runs from the main to the curb stop. From the back of the curb stop to your house, the pipe is yours. That split decides who pays for a yard leak, so we locate the break first and then tell you which side it is on.

Before anyone digs to reach it, Ohio811 asks for notice at least two working days ahead, and dialing 811 is free. We plan around that. Outside repairs get scheduled properly, and we work on stopping the loss from inside the house in the meantime.

Before we arrive

What to do while we are on the way

  • ✓Shut off the main.Cleveland Water says the master valve usually sits where the line enters the basement or crawl space. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  • ✓Open one low faucet.Draining the lines relieves pressure and slows whatever is leaking.
  • ✓Stay away from wet electrics.If water is near an outlet or panel, keep clear and shut that breaker only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground.
  • ✓Move valuables and take photos.Lift items off wet floors and photograph the damage, because your insurer will ask for it.
  • ✓Leave the walls alone.Cutting drywall yourself spreads water and erases the clues we use to trace the source.

If you smell gas or hear hissing near a gas appliance, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.
