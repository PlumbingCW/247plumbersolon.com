Safety first

Disposal leaking under the sink or jammed and humming

Turn the wall switch off, then unplug the unit or switch off its breaker before anyone reaches near it. Never put fingers in the drain, even when the motor sounds dead. A jammed disposal can restart the moment the jam clears.

If water is dripping, put a bucket under the cabinet and empty the space below. Run no water into the sink until we diagnose the leak. A leak from the top flange, the body, or the dishwasher hose each calls for a different repair.

Why a disposal hums, stalls, or leaks

Humming with no spinning

The motor has power but the blades are stuck. A jam, often a bone, a fruit pit, or a bottle cap, is the usual cause.

Silent when you flip the switch

The reset button on the bottom may have tripped, or the unit may have lost power. A dead motor is less common than a trip.

Water from the bottom or sides

A cracked housing or failed internal seal leaks from the body. This one usually ends in replacement.

Water around the top ring

A loose mounting ring or dried putty lets water seep into the cabinet. Often a repair, not a new unit.

Dishwasher drains into the disposal and backs up

A clogged dishwasher hose or an uncleared knockout plug can send dirty water back into the dishwasher.

Reset, repair, or replace the unit

Many disposal calls end with a reset or a cleared jam. The table shows when a new unit makes more sense than another repair.

Replacing or adding a disposal

A new disposal means removing the old mounting assembly, setting the sink flange with fresh putty or a gasket, and hanging the new unit from the ring. We reconnect the drain and the dishwasher hose, then knock out the dishwasher plug if the dishwasher drains through the unit. Power runs on a cord or a hardwired connection, depending on the cabinet.

Homes without a disposal can add one when there is room under the sink and an outlet or switch nearby. If new wiring or a bigger drain change is needed, we say so up front. Bigger changes, such as moving a sink drain, fall under plumbing installation.

When the sink backs up and the disposal gets blamed

Not every slow sink is the disposal's fault. If water rises in one bowl and the other side drains, the trap or branch line is usually clogged. If every fixture in the house is slow, the main line is the suspect, and a drain cleaning visit is the right next step.

Chemical drain cleaners can attack seals and gaskets in the disposal and are hard on plastic pipes. They also leave a hazard for the next person who opens the trap. We would rather clear the line mechanically.

What stays out of the disposal

Grease, coffee grounds, fibrous peels, potato skins, pasta, and rice are the items that most often leave a jam or a clog behind. Bones and fruit pits can nick blades. Run cold water while the unit works and for 15 seconds after it stops.

Local note

Solon pipes and what goes down the sink

Solon says the property owner is responsible for the lateral running from the house to the clean out, and city crews check the city main first. Grease that cools in a kitchen line builds a coating, and a repeat kitchen backup may be a lateral problem that needs sewer line repair, not a new disposal. If sewage backs up in the basement, call the city Service Department as the city advises, then call us.

Solon's permits guide lists no permit for fixture replacement, which includes swapping a disposal in the same spot. If the job moves plumbing, ask the Solon Building Department.
