Act first

Water spreading from a toilet right now

If the bowl is rising toward the rim, lift the tank lid and press the flapper down. Then reach behind the toilet and turn the small valve clockwise until it stops. That cuts the refill and usually ends the flood within seconds.

If the water is dark, smells like sewage, or other drains gurgle when you flush, stop using every fixture in the house. That pattern points to a blockage below the toilet, not a problem with the bowl. We check the toilet first, then trace the line if the bowl is not the cause.

Water at the base after a flush often means a failed wax ring or a loose closet bolt. Water dripping from the supply line means a bad connection, and the stop valve usually controls it. If no valve will turn, close the main shutoff where the line enters your house.

What the toilet is telling you

It keeps running after the flush

A worn flapper or tired fill valve lets water trickle into the bowl all day. It wastes water and shows up on the next bill.

The flush is weak or partial

Mineral buildup in the rim jets or a partial clog in the trap slows the swirl. Plunging helps only if the clog is close.

It rocks when you sit

A loose flange or slack bolts break the seal. Sewer gas and slow floor damage follow, so do not wait on this one.

Water pools around the base

Dry the floor, flush twice, and watch where the first drop appears. Bowl base, tank bolts, and supply line each leak in a different spot.

It gurgles after you flush

Air is fighting for a path through a restricted vent or a slow drain. The toilet may be fine and the pipes behind it may not be.

Bathroom floor already wet?

Describe what you see to the dispatcher and tell us whether you can reach the shutoff. We send the right seals, valves, and supply lines on the first trip.

Setting a new toilet the right way

An install starts with the old toilet coming out and the flange getting a close look. A flange that sits too low, cracks, or rusts through will make a new toilet leak no matter how good the bowl is. We replace the wax or foam seal every time, seat the bowl evenly, and tighten the bolts in turns so the porcelain does not crack.

After the bowl is set, we connect a fresh supply line, run several flushes, and check for weeping at every joint. A level bowl and a dry floor after the last flush are what we look for before we leave.

When the flange is the real problem

A repair ring or flange kit can rescue a cracked top ring when the pipe below is sound. If the pipe itself is broken or the floor underneath is soft, the job grows into plumbing installation work, and we tell you that before we open anything up.

Most toilets are worth repairing. The numbers change when the porcelain is damaged or the bowl is hard to keep clear.

Local note

Solon water, permits, and what is under your floor

Cleveland Water, which supplies Solon, states its water measures about 7 grains per gallon, or roughly 120 mg/L as calcium carbonate. That is moderately hard. Minerals can crust on flappers and clog rim jets, so a flush that has slowly weakened may need cleaning, not a new toilet.

Solon's Required Residential Permits Guide says replacing fixtures does not need a permit, while new installations and new plumbing lines do. If you plan to move the toilet to a new spot, call the Solon Building Department before work starts.

When several drains back up at once, the problem may be the line outside, not the toilet. Solon says the property owner is responsible for the lateral from the house to the clean out, so a repeated backup may call for sewer line repair or a drain cleaning visit.
