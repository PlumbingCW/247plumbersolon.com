A shop, office, or suite with no water or a flooded floor

Start by finding the main shutoff for your suite or building. In a multi-tenant property, that valve may be in a utility closet or a shared room, so ask your property manager if you do not have access. Keep the electrical panel and floor outlets dry, and move paper, inventory, and equipment up off the floor.

Tell the dispatcher the type of space, whether the water is clean or sewage, and who holds the key or lease. A clean-water break and a floor-drain backup need different equipment. Having a contact for the landlord or property manager speeds up access.

Local note

Registration, permits, and backflow in Solon

Solon requires contractors to register with the city every year and show a current Ohio plumbing license before they work in the city. Ask any plumber for proof, and verify a license through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board's online look-up.

Irrigation systems on commercial lots usually have backflow devices. Solon requires a permit for an irrigation backflow device, and Ohio's rule for backflow preventers calls for testing at least every 12 months. Cleveland Water asks for annual testing by a plumber, so check which of those applies to your meter before a test is due.

Tenants, owners, and who pays

Lease terms decide who pays for a plumbing failure, and we do not guess at them. Many leases make the landlord responsible for the supply and drain lines in the walls and the tenant responsible for fixtures inside the suite. Read the lease, call the property manager, and ask before the work starts so the invoice goes to the right place.

If you are the one paying, tell us when you call. We write the price in plain terms and explain what we found, so you can pass the details on. Projects that add fixtures, such as a new break room sink or restroom, fall under plumbing installation.

Why older Solon buildings call for a closer look

Solon's industrial growth began in the 1960s, when the first of its industrial parks was built off Solon Industrial Parkway. Buildings from that era and the decades after often have original supply and drain lines. If your building is that old, ask us to look at the pipe type and condition when we are on site.
